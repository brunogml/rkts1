Bodong Panchen Chokle Namgyal was one of the most prolific scholars of fifteenth-century Tibet, a polymath whose output rivals the great encyclopaedists of any tradition. Born in 1376 in the Bo dong region of southern Tibet, he trained at the major monastic centres of his day before establishing himself as the pre-eminent authority of the Bo dong school, a lineage that traces its doctrinal roots to the eleventh-century master Bo dong Phyogs glang.

His collected works — the bka' 'bum — extend to 137 volumes and are preserved in a print made at Bo dong E monastery. The corpus is exceptional in its breadth: it encompasses works on Madhyamaka and Pramāṇa philosophy, Kālacakra and other tantra, sādhana literature, grammar, poetics, history, and an extensive personal correspondence. Particularly celebrated is his Encyclopedia of Buddhist Doctrine (thub bstan rgya mtsho), running to many dozens of volumes and covering virtually the full range of Indo-Tibetan learning.

Despite his stature, Bodong Panchen remains comparatively understudied in Western scholarship, in part because of the sheer scale of his output and the relative scarcity of critical editions. This portal makes the digitised bka' 'bum accessible for the first time in an integrated reading environment, combining manuscript images from the Buddhist Digital Resource Center (BDRC) with etext transcriptions and a searchable handlist.
