How Similar Passages works
1 · Select a passage

Highlight any stretch of text in the etext viewer. A Find similar passages button appears at the bottom-right of the screen. Click it to start the search.

2 · Keywords are extracted

The selection is split into Tibetan syllables. Grammatical particles (case markers, connectives such as ba, dang, kyi, las, pa'i…) are filtered out using the rKTs particle list. The remaining syllables become your keywords, shown as yellow tags. Adjacent content-word pairs (bigrams, e.g. sangs rgyas) are also extracted as extra evidence and shown in blue.

3 · Refine keywords before searching

After the keyword preview appears, you can click any yellow keyword tag to deselect it — it will be struck through and excluded from the search. Click it again to re-enable it. This is useful when a syllable is too common or ambiguous to be a reliable search term. Once you have adjusted the keywords, click Reload to re-run the search.

4 · Choose a search mode

Two modes are available:

  • Bag of words — the keywords may appear anywhere in the 4-line window, in any order. Best for finding parallel passages where the same ideas are expressed with different word order or paraphrase. This is the default.
  • Phrase order — the keywords must appear in the same left-to-right order as in your selection (gaps are allowed). Stricter, and better for finding near-verbatim quotations or formulaic passages.

After switching mode, click Reload to apply it to the current selection.

5 · Results are ranked

All etext files of the current collection are scanned with a sliding window of 4 consecutive lines. Windows where at least 50% of the active keywords are found are kept. Results are sorted by keyword coverage; the score 6/8 means 6 of your 8 keywords were found in that passage. Up to 25 results are shown.

6 · Reading a result
  • The header bar shows volume, folio, and section.
  • The D236 badge links to the catalog entry for that text.
  • e-text opens the full etext; image opens the manuscript viewer at that folio.
  • Matched keywords are highlighted in yellow in the passage snippet.
Limitations

The search is limited to the current collection's etext files. Spelling variants, abbreviated forms, and syllables split across lines may be missed. Passages with very short selections (fewer than 3–4 content words) will produce many false positives.
